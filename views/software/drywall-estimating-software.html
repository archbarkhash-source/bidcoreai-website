What it is

Why Drywall Is Priced Per Square Foot and Lost Per Assembly

Drywall estimating looks like an area problem and behaves like a multiplication problem. The plan gives you wall length; the wall type legend gives you everything that matters. How many layers of board on each side. What thickness and type — regular, type X, mould-resistant, abuse-resistant, glass-mat. Whether the assembly is rated, and therefore how it must be detailed and firestopped. What finish level the specification requires on that surface. Multiply length by height and you get an area; multiply that by the wrong assembly and you get a number that is confidently wrong by a factor.

BidcoreAI measures interiors by wall type, taking the assembly definition from the legend. A measured run of wall converts to board area with the layer count applied per side, board type carried per layer, and openings deducted by rule — the rule being one you set, since drywall commonly ignores small openings that a painting estimate would deduct.

Finish level is treated as a cost driver rather than a note. Level 3, level 4 and level 5 differ substantially in labor and material, and level 5 across a large gloss-painted lobby is an entirely different proposition from level 4 in a back-of-house corridor. The specification usually says which applies where, and reading it is part of the takeoff rather than an optional refinement.

Because framing and board share the same wall, both come out of one measurement. Studs at spacing, track, headers and backing on one side; board, tape, compound, corner bead, insulation, acoustic sealant and firestopping on the other. Ceilings, soffits, bulkheads, shafts and column wraps are measured as their own quantities, which matters because ceiling and soffit work runs at a different productivity from flat wall and gets under-carried whenever it is priced as though it were the same thing.

How a Drywall Estimate Comes Together

Legend, plans, ceilings, specification — in that order, because each one changes the next.

1
Upload plans, legend and schedules
Floor plans, wall type legend, reflected ceiling plans, sections, interior elevations, finish schedules and the Division 09 specification sections.
2
Wall types read
Each type is extracted with its layers, board types, rating and stud spacing, and stored as a property that every measurement of that type inherits.
3
Partitions measured
Wall runs measured per type across each level, heights taken from the sections, openings deducted by your rule rather than a default someone else chose.
4
Ceilings and soffits measured
Reflected ceiling plans measured for gypsum ceilings, soffits and bulkheads, which are kept separate because they run at different productivity.
5
Board and finishing calculated
Areas convert to board area by layer, then to sheet counts by size, with tape, compound and bead quantified by joint and corner length.
6
Finish levels applied
The specification is read for finish level by surface, and levels 3, 4 and 5 are applied with their own labor and material rather than one blended finishing rate.
7
Priced and delivered
Framing, board, finishing, insulation and firestopping priced against your own rates and exported to Excel with a marked-up plan set behind every quantity.
