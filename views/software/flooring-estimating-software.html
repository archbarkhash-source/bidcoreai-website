What it is

Floor Area Is the Start of a Flooring Estimate, Not the End

Flooring is deceptively simple to measure and easy to under-price. The floor plan gives an area; the finish schedule gives eleven different materials spread across it; and the cost per square foot varies by a factor of ten between broadloom carpet in an open office and quarry tile with an epoxy grout in a kitchen. Then there is everything that is not floor area at all: base, transitions, thresholds, stair nosings, substrate preparation and the moisture testing without which the manufacturer's warranty does not exist.

BidcoreAI measures flooring room by room and maps each room to its finish from the schedule. That structure matters — a room is the unit of installation, the unit of base measurement and the unit the finish schedule is written in. Area is measured net of walls and fixed elements, and the perimeter is captured at the same time, since base length is a perimeter problem that most area-based takeoffs never answer.

Material is calculated with waste that reflects reality. Broadloom carpet from a 12-foot roll in an irregular room wastes very differently from carpet tile, which wastes differently from a plank floor laid diagonally, which wastes differently again from a tile with a pattern repeat and a 45-degree layout. Applying a flat 10% to all of them is fast and wrong in both directions.

Preparation is treated as scope. Grinding, patching, self-levelling, moisture vapour testing, adhesive removal and old floor demolition are quantified separately, because on renovation work they routinely exceed the installed cost of the new floor and are the most common omission in a quick bid. Transitions, thresholds, reducers and stair treatment are counted by type rather than assumed proportional to area.

Scope

What the Software Takes Off on a Flooring Package

Floor plans, the finish schedule, floor transitions plan and interior elevations.

Floor Areas

Measured room by room and mapped to the finish schedule rather than as one floor plate.

  • Net area by room and finish type
  • Deductions for fixed elements
  • Pattern direction and layout
  • Level and zone grouping
Base and Perimeter

Perimeter captured with the area, because base length is a different measurement entirely.

  • Base length by room and material
  • Rubber, vinyl, wood and tile base
  • Corners, ends and returns
  • Coved base at wet areas
Transitions

Counted by type where finishes meet, which is where the fussy labor lives.

  • Thresholds and reducers by type
  • Transition strips and edge trim
  • Expansion and movement joints
  • Door-line transitions
Substrate Preparation

Its own scope, and on a renovation typically the difference between profit and loss.

  • Grinding, patching and skim
  • Self-levelling by area and depth
  • Moisture vapour testing
  • Adhesive removal and demolition
Stairs and Specials

Measured per tread and riser, since a stair is not a square-foot problem.

  • Treads, risers and landings
  • Stringers and nosings
  • Ramps and sloped areas
  • Access floor and raised systems
Material and Waste

Calculated by product form, roll width and pattern repeat rather than a flat percentage.

  • Roll goods by width and seam plan
  • Tile and plank by carton
  • Pattern repeat and diagonal layout
  • Adhesive, grout, setting materials

What Changes on a Flooring Bid

The perimeter and the substrate stop being invisible.

Base Actually Measured

Perimeter captured with area means base is a measured quantity rather than a guess derived from square footage, which is never right in irregular plans.

Prep Priced Up Front

Self-levelling, grinding and adhesive removal on a renovation can exceed the new floor. Measuring them is the difference between a bid and a hope.

Waste That Reflects Layout

A diagonal plank layout in small rooms wastes nothing like carpet tile in open plan. Per-product waste stops one subsidising the other.

Transitions Counted

Every finish change is a piece of trim and a slow ten minutes. Counting them is how a fit-out with 40 rooms gets priced honestly.

Schedule-Accurate Materials

Mapping rooms to the finish schedule catches the areas where the specification changed and the plan did not.

Orders in Purchase Units

Cartons, rolls and pails with attic stock included means the order matches the job rather than the estimate.
