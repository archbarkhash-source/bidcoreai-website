What it is

Demolition Is a Logistics Estimate Wearing Work Boots

Demolition looks like the least technical package on a project and behaves like one of the least forgiving. The labor to take something apart is only part of the cost. The rest is disposal — how many cubic yards of debris, how many tons, how many containers, how many haul cycles, which waste streams have to be separated, and what the tipping fee is for each. A selective interior demolition bid that got the labor right and the container count wrong will still lose money, and the container count follows from the volume and density of what comes out, which is a takeoff problem.

BidcoreAI measures demolition by element and material from the demolition plans. Walls by type and area, ceilings by area, flooring by area and material, casework and fixtures by count, MEP removals by run and device, structural removals by volume and material. Each element carries a material density, so measured quantities convert into disposal volume and tonnage rather than stopping at square feet.

Selective demolition is measured room by room, because that is how it is performed and how it is phased in an occupied building. Protection scope — temporary partitions, floor and surface protection, dust control, negative air, path protection — is quantified rather than allowed for, and on a fit-out in an operating facility it is frequently a larger number than the demolition itself.

Where the drawings or the specification indicate hazardous materials, the abatement interface is flagged rather than swallowed. Asbestos, lead paint, mould and contaminated soils belong to a licensed abatement scope, and the estimate should say clearly what has been excluded and where the boundary lies. Structural removals carry their shoring and their sequencing, which are engineering questions that appear in a bid as costs and as risk.

Interior Removals

  • Flooring by material and area

Structural Removals

Volume and material by element, with the shoring and sequencing that go with them.

  • Concrete removal by volume
  • Masonry and CMU removal

MEP Removals

Runs and devices counted, since MEP demolition is slow and rarely drawn completely.

  • Duct, pipe and conduit runs
  • Fixtures, devices and equipment
  • Panels, gear and controls
  • Capping, isolation and make-safe
Disposal

Converted from removal quantities using material density, by waste stream.

  • Volume and tonnage by material
  • Container size and count
  • Haul cycles and tipping fees
  • Recycling and diversion targets
Protection and Temporary Works

Often the larger cost on a fit-out inside an operating building.

  • Temporary partitions and barriers
  • Floor, wall and surface protection
  • Dust control and negative air
  • Shoring and temporary support
Abatement Interface

Flagged and bounded rather than absorbed into the demolition price.

  • Asbestos and lead identified
  • Survey references and locations
  • Scope boundary written down
  • Exclusions stated explicitly
