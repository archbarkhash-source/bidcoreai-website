What it is

The Plant Schedule Is Only the First Page of the Bid

Landscape estimating starts easily. The planting plan and plant schedule tell you what to count: trees by species and caliper, shrubs by size and container, groundcover by area and spacing. Counting them accurately is straightforward work. What is not straightforward is everything that has to exist before a plant goes in the ground and everything that has to happen after it does — soil preparation and amendment, bed excavation, drainage, staking, mulch, irrigation, establishment watering and the maintenance period that runs until the warranty ends.

BidcoreAI counts the planting from the schedule and reconciles it against the plan, then measures the beds and lawns as areas so soil amendment, mulch and sod come out as real volumes at their specified depths. A four-inch mulch depth over an acre of beds is a substantial quantity that gets carried as a lump sum far more often than it gets measured.

Irrigation is estimated by system rather than by area. Heads are counted by type and coverage, zones are counted from the valve schedule, mainline and lateral pipe are measured by size, and controllers, sensors, backflow prevention and wiring are counted individually. Where the design is performance-based, coverage is derived from the head spacing and the area rather than left as an allowance.

Hardscape is measured the way a paving package is: by area and section, with base depth, bedding, edge restraint and jointing quantified by layer, and pavers, walls and steps counted or measured in the units they are bought in. And the maintenance and establishment period is priced as scope, since a twelve-month warranty with monthly visits is a labor commitment that outlasts every other trade on the project.
