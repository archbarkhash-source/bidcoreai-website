What it is

A Foot of Wall Is Not a Unit of Cost

Metal framing is measured in linear feet of wall and priced in something else entirely. A 10-foot-high 20-gauge shaft wall at 16 inches on centre with a deflection head and full-height bracing has almost nothing in common, cost-wise, with an 8-foot 25-gauge office partition at 24 inches on centre — yet a linear-foot rate treats them as the same product. That is the gap this software is built to close.

BidcoreAI measures partitions by wall type. Each type carries its stud size, gauge, spacing, height and head condition from the wall type legend, so a measured length converts directly into stud counts, track lengths and the connection details that go with them. Openings generate their own framing — jambs, headers, sills and the extra studs a wide opening needs — rather than being deducted from the wall and forgotten.

The items that make framing slow are counted too. Deflection track and slip connections at structural interfaces. Bridging and bracing rows by height. Backing for millwork, grab bars, TVs, handrails and equipment, taken from the interior elevations and details, which is the scope most often discovered on site rather than in the estimate. Fire-rated and acoustic assemblies carry their own detailing and their own labor.

Because framing and drywall share the same walls, the same measurement produces both. One pass over the plan set yields the framing package and the board, tape, insulation and finish quantities — which is the practical reason drywall contractors end up doing the framing takeoff twice in most workflows, and why doing it once is worth this much attention.

Scope

What the Software Measures on a Framing Package

Read from the wall type legend, the floor plans, the interior elevations and the framing details.

Partitions by Type

Wall lengths measured per wall type, with size, gauge, spacing and height taken from the legend.

  • Length by wall type and level
  • Stud size, gauge and spacing
  • Wall height and head condition
  • Rated and acoustic assemblies flagged
Stud and Track Counts

Converted from length at the real spacing, with corners, intersections and ends counted.

  • Studs at 12, 16 or 24 inch centres
  • Top and bottom track by size
  • Corner, tee and end studs
  • Stud lengths by wall height
Opening Framing

Jambs, headers and sills generated from the door, window and borrowed-light openings.

  • Jamb studs by opening width
  • Headers and header depth
  • Sills and sub-sills
  • Extra studs at wide openings
Deflection and Connections

The structural interface details that decide how long a wall actually takes to build.

  • Deflection track and slip clips
  • Slotted track and vertical movement
  • Anchors and fasteners by substrate
  • Bracing to structure above
Bracing and Backing

Bridging rows and in-wall backing taken from the details and interior elevations.

  • Bridging and bracing rows by height
  • Backing for millwork and equipment
  • Grab bar, TV and handrail backing
  • Blocking at mechanical penetrations
Ceilings and Soffits

Suspended framing, furring and soffit framing measured as their own quantities.

  • Suspended ceiling framing
  • Soffit and bulkhead framing
  • Furring channels and hat channel
  • Shaft wall and chase framing
