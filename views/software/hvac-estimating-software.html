What it is

Three Different Estimates Wearing One Name

An HVAC package is really three estimates sharing a cover sheet. The equipment is a counted list off the schedules with connection, rigging and start-up scope attached. The ductwork is a fabrication estimate priced by weight of metal, gauge, seam type and lining. The piping is a mechanical estimate priced by size, material and fitting count. Each has its own units and its own labor logic, and folding them into a single square-foot rate is how a mechanical bid ends up either uncompetitive or unprofitable, with no way to tell which until the job is running.

BidcoreAI keeps them separate. Equipment — air handlers, rooftop units, VAV boxes, fan coils, pumps, chillers, boilers, exhaust fans — is counted from the schedules by tag, with electrical connection, structural support, curbs, rigging, start-up and commissioning carried per unit. Lead times are noted, because on current schedules a rooftop unit delivery is a project constraint rather than a procurement detail.

Ductwork is measured by run and size, then converted to pounds of metal by gauge according to SMACNA construction class, with fittings counted rather than added as a blanket percentage. Liner and external insulation are calculated by surface area from the same measurement. That is what makes the number quotable by a fabrication shop, and comparable against a shop's own price, instead of being a linear-foot figure only the estimator can interpret.

Piping is measured by system — chilled water, heating hot water, condenser, refrigerant, condensate — by size and material, with fittings, valves, hangers and insulation quantified. Controls are scoped separately by point count and device type, along with dampers, sensors, test and balance and commissioning, all of which are routinely under-carried because they appear on a different set of drawings than the ductwork does.

Scope

What the Software Takes Off on an HVAC Package

Mechanical plans, equipment schedules, duct layouts, piping diagrams and the controls drawings.

Equipment

Counted by schedule tag, with the connection and start-up scope each unit brings with it.

  • AHUs, RTUs and split systems
  • VAV boxes, fan coils and unit heaters
  • Chillers, boilers and pumps
  • Curbs, rigging, start-up and lead times
Ductwork

Measured by run and size, converted to pounds of metal by gauge and construction class.

  • Rectangular duct by size and gauge
  • Round and oval duct by diameter
  • Pounds of metal by construction class
  • Liner, wrap and external insulation
Duct Fittings and Devices

Counted individually, because a fitting-heavy layout costs far more than its linear feet suggest.

  • Elbows, transitions and takeoffs
  • Dampers, fire and smoke dampers
  • Diffusers, registers and grilles
  • Flex duct runs and connections
Hydronic Piping

Measured by system, size and material with fittings, valves and specialties counted.

  • Chilled and heating hot water
  • Condenser and glycol systems
  • Refrigerant and condensate
  • Valves, specialties and hangers
Controls and BAS

Scoped by point count and device type rather than carried as a percentage of the mechanical value.

  • Control points by device
  • Sensors, actuators and valves
  • Panels, wiring and integration
  • Sequences and graphics
Commissioning and Balance

The end-of-job scope that is real work and is almost always under-carried at bid.

  • Test and balance by terminal
  • Commissioning support hours
  • Duct leakage testing
  • O&M documentation and training

Benefits

What Changes on a Mechanical Bid

Each of the three sub-estimates gets priced in its own currency.

Duct a Shop Can Quote

Pounds of metal by gauge and class is the number a fabrication shop prices from. Linear feet is a number only the estimator can interpret.

Fittings Counted

A tight mechanical room with forty fittings per hundred feet costs nothing like an open ceiling run. Counting them is the only way that shows up.

Controls Actually Priced

Point-count scoping replaces the percentage-of-mechanical habit, which is consistently low on projects with dense terminal units.

Lead Times Known at Bid

Equipment delivery is now a schedule driver. Capturing it during the estimate turns it into a planning input rather than a mid-job surprise.

Commissioning Carried

Test and balance, duct leakage testing and commissioning support are real hours that get remembered after award if they are not counted before it.

Shop and Field Separated

Fabrication and installation productivity differ. Estimating them separately is what makes the labor number reviewable.
