What it is

Roofing Is Priced by the Square and Lost at the Edges

A commercial roof estimate has an easy part and a hard part. The easy part is the field: measure the area, divide by a hundred, price it per square. The hard part is everything at a boundary — parapet flashing, edge metal, counterflashing, expansion joints, curbs, drains, vents, pipe penetrations, equipment supports and the walk pads that connect them. Two roofs of identical area can differ by 50% in cost depending on how many of those exist, and the drawing that shows them is usually the roof plan nobody measures carefully because the field area was so easy to get.

BidcoreAI measures roof area by system, level and slope, then measures the perimeter and every flashing condition by length and type. Penetrations are counted individually by type and size from the roof plan and the mechanical drawings, which is where half of them actually are — a roof plan rarely shows every curb the mechanical schedule implies, and reconciling the two is part of the takeoff.

Insulation is quantified by layer and R-value, including tapered systems. Tapered insulation is a genuinely difficult quantity — it is a volume problem disguised as an area problem, and the difference between an average thickness and a real layout can be a large number on a big roof. Cover board, vapour barrier and adhesive or fastener patterns are carried per layer, with fastening density taken from the wind uplift requirements rather than assumed.

Tear-off is estimated as its own scope with its own disposal cost, by existing system and number of layers, along with deck inspection and repair allowances. On a re-roof this is frequently the largest single variable in the bid, and it is the one most exposed to what is found once the old roof comes off — which is why it belongs in the estimate as a stated assumption rather than a hopeful lump sum.

Roof Areas

Measured by system, level and slope, with each roof area kept separate rather than totalled.

Insulation

By layer and R-value, including the tapered layouts that are a volume problem in disguise.

  • Base and cover layers by thickness
  • Tapered systems by layout
  • Cover board and vapour barrier
  • Fastening density from uplift
Tear-Off and Deck

Its own scope with its own disposal, and the largest variable on a re-roof.

  • Existing system and layer count
