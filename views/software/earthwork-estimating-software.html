What it is

Earthwork Is a Difference Between Two Surfaces

Every other trade measures something that exists on a drawing. Earthwork measures the difference between two things that do not: the existing ground surface and the proposed one. Cut is where the proposed grade is below existing, fill is where it is above, and the estimate is the volume between them — adjusted for topsoil strip, for subgrade preparation below slabs and pavements, and for the fact that soil does not occupy the same volume in the ground, in a truck and compacted back into an embankment.

BidcoreAI builds both surfaces from the drawings. Existing contours and spot elevations from the topographic survey; proposed contours, spot grades and pavement sections from the grading plan. The volume between them is computed by grid or by cross-section, then adjusted per material for shrink and swell so that a cubic yard of cut becomes the right number of truckloads and the right number of compacted yards in fill.

The balance is what matters commercially. Two sites with identical cut volumes are entirely different jobs if one balances on site and the other exports twenty thousand cubic yards to a landfill fourteen miles away. Import and export are calculated separately with haul distance, cycle time and disposal cost, because on most projects the haul is a larger number than the excavation.

Structural excavation is kept apart from mass grading. Footing and foundation excavation, trench excavation by depth and width, over-excavation and replacement, rock provisions and dewatering are estimated with their own productivity and their own risk, since they are done with different equipment by different crews under different conditions. Where soils reports flag unsuitable material or a high water table, the estimate carries the assumption explicitly rather than absorbing it into a unit rate.

What the Software Takes Off on an Earthwork Package

Topographic survey, grading plan, pavement sections, foundation plan and the geotechnical report.

Mass Grading

Cut and fill volumes computed between existing and proposed surfaces.

  • Existing surface from survey data
  • Proposed surface from grading plan
  • Cut and fill by grid or section
  • Balance and net import or export
Topsoil

Stripped, stockpiled and respread — three operations, not one line.

  • Strip depth and area
  • Stockpile volume and location
  • Respread depth at landscape areas
  • Surplus disposal or import
Shrink and Swell

Applied by material so bank, loose and compacted volumes stay distinct.

  • Bank to loose for hauling
  • Bank to compacted for fill
  • Material-specific factors
  • Subgrade compaction requirements
Structural Excavation

Foundations, trenches and pits, priced apart from mass grading.

  • Footing and foundation excavation
  • Trench by depth, width and length
  • Over-excavation and replacement
  • Backfill and compaction
Haul and Disposal

Frequently the largest number in the package, and rarely the one measured first.

  • Haul distance and cycle time
  • Truck counts and load size
  • Import source and material cost
  • Disposal fees and tipping
Site Conditions and Risk

Taken from the geotechnical report and carried as stated assumptions.

  • Rock provisions and blasting
  • Dewatering and groundwater
  • Unsuitable material and undercut
  • Erosion control and stabilisation

Workflow

How an Earthwork Estimate Comes Together

Two surfaces, one difference, and then the trucks.

1
Upload survey and grading plans
Topographic survey, grading plan, pavement and slab sections, foundation plan, utility plans and the geotechnical report.
2
Surfaces built
Existing contours and spot elevations, then proposed contours and spot grades, are extracted and built into two surfaces that can be differenced.
3
Cut and fill computed
Volumes are calculated between the surfaces by grid or cross-section, with the method and grid size recorded so the result can be checked or re-run.
4
Topsoil and subgrade adjusted
Strip depth is removed before the comparison, and subgrade preparation below slabs and pavements is added from the section details.
5
Factors applied
Shrink and swell are applied per material so bank, loose and compacted volumes remain distinct, giving correct truck counts and correct fill quantities.
6
Haul and import priced
Net import or export is calculated with haul distance, cycle time, truck size, material cost and disposal fees priced as their own lines.
7
Structural excavation added
Foundation and trench excavation, backfill, compaction and any rock or dewatering provisions are priced separately and exported with the mass grading.
Benefits

What Changes on an Earthwork Bid

The estimate starts answering the question that decides the job: where does the dirt go?

A Real Balance

Knowing net import or export rather than gross cut is what separates a site that pays for itself from one that spends a fortune on trucking.

Volumes That Convert

Keeping bank, loose and compacted volumes distinct means the truck count and the fill quantity are both right — using one number for both guarantees one of them is wrong.

Haul Priced Properly

Distance and cycle time drive the largest cost in most earthwork packages, and a per-yard rate hides them completely.

Risk Stated

Rock, groundwater and unsuitable material carried as visible assumptions gives you something to negotiate with rather than a number to defend.

Structural Work Separated

Trench and foundation excavation at mass-grading rates is a standing underbid, because it is slower work with smaller equipment.

Regrades Handled

When the pad elevation changes, comparing the surfaces gives you the volume difference directly instead of a second full takeoff.
