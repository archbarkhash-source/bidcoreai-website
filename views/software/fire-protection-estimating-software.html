What it is

The Hazard Classification Sets the Whole Bid

Fire protection is one of the few trades where the specification determines the quantity before anyone measures anything. The hazard classification — light, ordinary group 1 or 2, extra hazard — sets the design density, and the density sets the maximum coverage area per sprinkler head. Change the classification of a storage area and the head count in that area changes with it, along with the pipe sizes feeding it. An estimator who counts heads off a drawing without checking the classification against the specification is counting somebody else's design.

BidcoreAI reads the specification and the design criteria alongside the drawings. Head counts are derived from area and hazard classification where a layout is not yet drawn, and counted directly where it is, with the basis shown either way. Head types are separated — pendant, upright, sidewall, concealed, dry, extended coverage — because their costs and their installation times differ materially, and because a concealed head in a finished ceiling is a different job from an upright in a warehouse.

Pipe is measured by system and size: wet, dry, pre-action, deluge and antifreeze loops each carry their own material, fittings and labor. Risers, standpipes, control valve assemblies, inspector test connections, drains and fire department connections are counted as assemblies rather than reduced to feet of pipe. Fire pumps, jockey pumps, controllers and tanks are counted from the schedules with their power, structural and start-up interfaces noted.

The alarm side is estimated by device count — initiating devices, notification appliances, panels, annunciators and the wiring between them — with the interface to the electrical contractor stated explicitly. That interface is where fire protection scope most often falls between two bidders, and writing it down at estimate time is considerably cheaper than discovering it at the coordination meeting.

Benefits

What Changes on a Fire Protection Bid

Mostly, that the design basis is checked before the count is trusted.

Counts Tied to the Criteria

Deriving head counts from hazard classification catches the case where the drawn layout does not match the specification — before it becomes a rework item.

Ceiling Conditions Priced

A concealed head in a finished ceiling and an upright over racking are different installations. Applying separate productivity is what stops one subsidising the other.

Assemblies Not Dissolved

Riser and valve assemblies counted as assemblies means the labor that lives in them is priced instead of vanishing into feet of pipe.

The Alarm Interface Stated

Writing down what the electrical contractor is carrying is the cheapest possible way to avoid a gap in coverage at the coordination meeting.

Seismic Bracing Carried

Sway bracing quantified where the seismic category requires it rather than remembered during installation, when it is a change order.

Testing Included

Hydrostatic testing, flushing, inspection and commissioning are real hours, and counting them at bid is the only time it costs nothing.
